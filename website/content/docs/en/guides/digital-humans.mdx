Digital humans aren't a separate area in Mosael — they are **two combinations of inputs inside video generation**:

| | You give it | You get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Talking photo** | A portrait + a voice-over | A video of that face speaking the voice-over |
| **Lip-sync** | An existing video + a new voice-over | The same footage with the person's mouth matching the new audio |

The **length follows the voice-over**: these models take no duration setting, and the UI says "duration follows the driving
audio". If the audio is longer or shorter than the model accepts, you're told when you submit ("the driving audio is 72 s;
this model takes at most 20 s") instead of a job that is bound to fail.

## Which models

After you [connect a provider](/docs/guides/providers), the models appear in every model picker — only the ones that declare
talking photo or lip-sync.

| Provider | Model | What it does | Audio length | Needs object storage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alibaba Bailian | wan2.2-s2v | Talking photo (480P / 720P; checks for a clear frontal face first) | 1–20 s | No |
| Alibaba Bailian | videoretalk | Lip-sync | 2–120 s | No |
| Volcengine Jimeng | OmniHuman 1.5 | Talking photo (720p / 1080p; an optional prompt steers motion and camera) | 1–59 s | Yes |
| Kling | Kling Avatar | Talking photo (optional prompt) | 2–300 s | No |
| Kling | Kling Lip-sync | Lip-sync (finds the face on screen longest first) | 2–60 s | For the source video |
| HeyGen | Avatar IV | Talking photo (optional motion prompt) | Up to 10 min | No |
| HeyGen | Lip-sync | Lip-sync (mp4 / webm source) | — | No |
| Hedra | Character-3 | Talking photo (aspect ratio picked from the image) | Up to 10 min | No |

The providers that need object storage only accept public links, so local images and audio are uploaded to the
[object storage you set up in plugins](/docs/guides/plugins) first. All of these were built from the providers' official
documentation; the first time you use one, keep an eye on the error message — it repeats what the provider said.

## Where to use them

- **Character assets**: "Make it talk" on the asset page — write a script, it is voiced with the character's own voice, and
  its front view (or the first image in pick order) becomes the talking photo. Character cells on boards have it too.
- **Boards**: "Make it talk" on image cells (pick a voice in the panel, or wire in an audio cell) and "Lip-sync" on video
  cells (audio from an upstream audio cell, or voiced from a script on the spot). The panel lets you pick the model;
  "Resolution" is under advanced options and lists only the tiers the chosen model has.
- **AI Studio**: pick a talking-photo or lip-sync model and attach the portrait / source video plus a driving audio.
- **Agent**: it knows how — voice first, then hand the face and that audio to a talking-photo model (`generate_video`).
- **Workflows**: the "Make it talk" (character / image), "Lip-sync", "Long script to segments" and "Dub lip-sync" nodes.

## Templates

The template library has three ready-made graphs:

- **Script → talking presenter**: one frontal face and a script. The script is voiced sentence by sentence, neighbouring
  sentences are grouped up to what the model accepts in one go (seams fall between sentences), each group is spoken by the
  face, the clips are laid end to end on the timeline, captions follow the measured voice-over, and the result is exported.
  A group shorter than the model's minimum (Kling starts at 2 s, and a lone "OK." can form its own group) is padded with a
  little silence; later groups shift accordingly.
- **Video translation · lip-sync**: after transcribing, translating and dubbing sentence by sentence, the speaker's mouth
  is matched to the translated voice-over. The source is cut into chunks the model accepts, at gaps between sentences;
  chunks without dubbing reuse the original for free. The fixed footage goes on a new top video track; the source is untouched.
- **Product → on-camera presenter pitch**: pick a character from the asset library as the host; it opens and closes on
  camera, with product shots in between.

## Consent

A digital human needs the pictured person's consent. Mosael checks three things, **the same way at every entry point**
(AI Studio, boards, the agent, workflows, scheduled tasks):

1. **Every generation is confirmed**: any generation with driving audio needs "I have the pictured person's consent" ticked —
   it's you, or they agreed separately. The agent has to pass that declaration too.
2. **Character declarations**: when the face is a reference image of a character asset that is a **real person**, it needs
   "This is me" or "The person has given consent" (fictional characters don't). Add it on the [asset page](/docs/guides/assets).
3. **Cloned-voice declarations**: when the driving audio was voiced with a cloned voice, that voice must say whose it is
   (you, consented, or fictional). Voices created before this was asked are "undeclared"; declare them in the voice
   library before using them for a digital human.

Whatever is missing is named when you submit — which character, which voice. The agent says so when it opens the
confirmation card, not after you approve it.

## AI-generated label

When an exported video contains digital-human clips, "AI-generated" is shown at the start and in the top-right corner by
default. You can turn it off in the export dialog, which then states that deep-synthesis and AI-labelling regulations
require published digital-human content to be clearly labelled and that the publisher is responsible once it's off; the
next export has it on again. The MP4 always carries an AIGC marker, regardless of the on-screen label.

## Cost

Talking photos and lip-sync are metered by the **actual length** of the output (which is the voice-over's length). Bailian
wan2.2-s2v (0.5 CNY/s at 480P) and videoretalk (0.08 CNY/s) can be pre-filled from their list prices in Admin → Cost
rules; the other providers either show prices only after signing in or bill in credits, so enter your account's prices by hand.
