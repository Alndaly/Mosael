The media library answers "what is this file"; the asset library answers "who is this". An **asset** is a named thing — a
character, a location, a prop — whose reference images are images from your media library: the asset only points at them,
it never copies the files. The same character usually appears across projects, so assets belong to the **workspace**, not
to a project; tags keep them organised.

Open **Assets** next to **Media** in the sidebar. Tabs at the top switch between characters, locations and props; each card
is an asset's cover.

## Create an asset

Click **New character** (or location, prop), give it a name, and you land on its detail page:

- **Description**: for people — who this is, where it comes from.
- **Prompt descriptor**: for models — appearance, clothing, materials. Whenever the asset is `@`-mentioned in a generation,
  this text is appended to the prompt.
- **Tags**: separated by commas; they are how you find things across projects.
- **Kind-specific fields**: characters have a **voice** (from your voice library), a **blockout color** for their stand-in
  figure in 3D blockouts, and **real or fictional** with a consent declaration; locations have a linked **3D scene** and
  "time / weather / light"; props have their **3D model**.

Fields save when you leave them.

## The reference wall

The left side of the detail page is a wall of reference images, each labelled with its angle or purpose: front, side, back,
turnaround, close-up, full body, expression, concept, detail.

- **Add from media library**: open the media panel on the right and click **+**, or drag a thumbnail onto the wall.
- **Drop files on the wall**: they are imported into the media library first, then attached.
- **Change the angle** with the dropdown under each image. **Set as cover**, **move earlier / later** and **remove from
  references** are in its **⋯** menu; you can also drag images to reorder.
- Without a cover, the card uses the first reference image.

**Deleting an asset never deletes media**: the images stay in the media library. **Deleting media** removes that reference
from any asset that used it; the asset itself stays, and its detail page says which image went missing until you dismiss the
note. The delete confirmation in the media library mentions this too.

The media detail view gains a **Used by assets** row, and right-clicking an image in the media library offers **Set as an
asset's reference…** (optionally as its cover).

## Variants

Winter clothes, period costume, the teenage version of the same character are **variants** hanging under it. A variant
inherits its parent's prompt descriptor and voice; only the references and the outfit description change — "Zhang San ·
Winter" is the same person as "Zhang San". A variant without references of its own uses its parent's in generations.
Deleting an asset that has variants asks you to confirm that they go too.

## Real people and consent

A character is declared **fictional** or **a real person**. A real person needs "This is me" or "The person has given
consent" — every option explains what it means on screen. The server records who declared it and when. A real person
without a declaration can't be used for the digital-human features that come later ("make it talk"); this is the separate
consent that deep-synthesis regulations require.

## @-mention assets in generations

When you write a prompt in a board's generation item, the `@` menu lists characters, locations and props from the asset
library (with cover and kind) alongside media; AI Studio has an **@ Asset** button under its prompt; the workflow **AI
generate** node has an **Assets** field; the agent's `generate_image` / `generate_video` take `entity_ids`. All four do the
same thing:

1. The asset's **prompt descriptor** is appended to the prompt (a variant contributes its parent's descriptor plus its own).
2. Reference images are **picked in order**: turnaround > front > full body > the rest. With several assets mentioned, picks
   take turns so the first one doesn't use up every slot.
3. **Only the selected model's description decides how many fit**: how many reference images it takes and whether it can
   combine them with an attached first/last frame. A model without a known description gets none — nothing is guessed.
4. **What didn't fit is reported**: the AI Studio history and the board both say how many images were attached, how many
   were left out, and why.

Models that build a reusable subject first (such as Kling) get one subject per mentioned character, built from its reference
images with the front view first. The subject's name is derived from the image contents, so using the same character again
finds the same subject instead of creating a duplicate.

## The asset item on boards

**Add → Reference → Asset** places an **asset item** on a board: cover, name and a kind badge. Connect it into an image or
video generation item and it works exactly like an `@` mention; in the prompt's `@` menu, connected assets come first.
When you share a board to the community, the asset item shows as an image (its cover and name); the asset's prompt
descriptor and other references are not shared with the board.

## Where it's used

The bottom of the detail page lists **where the asset is used**: boards that hold its asset item or mention it in a
prompt, generations that named it, and workflows whose generation nodes use it. Click a row to go there.

## Share to the community

**Share to community** next to the asset's name publishes the asset **together with its variants** (connect an account in
**Settings → Community account** first). What goes out: the image references (with their angles, cover and order), the
description, prompt descriptor, tags and the fields that can be public (blockout colour, time of day). **Voices, 3D scenes
and models, the consent declaration itself and any local ids stay on your machine**, and video references aren't included.
Fill in a title, summary and tags and publish; it goes live right away. Afterwards the button reads **Publish new version** —
still the same community entry at the same link. The line under the name shows its community status (published / in review;
click to open it in your browser).

A **real person** needs "This is me" or "The person has given consent" declared locally, and you tick **The person agreed to
this being public** again when you share. The community reviews it before it goes public. Someone whose likeness is misused
can report the asset page (reason "Uses someone’s likeness without consent"). Variants aren't shared on their own.

## Import from the community

**Import from community** at the top of the assets page: paste a community asset link (or its short name), or browse and
search characters, locations and props below and click one. Importing **doesn't need a community account**. Every reference
is checked against the hash in the bundle after download — if one doesn't match, nothing is created. The checked images
become media in this workspace (source "community"), then the asset, its variants, angles and cover are created, and the asset
remembers which community entry and version it came from: the line under its name reads "From the community · v1", and when
a newer version appears you're told and can import it with one click.

An imported **real person** gets the consent state **awaiting your confirmation**: its publisher declared the person's consent,
but nobody on this machine has confirmed it. Until then it can't be used for digital humans; once you have the person's
permission too, pick one of the options under Consent declaration.
